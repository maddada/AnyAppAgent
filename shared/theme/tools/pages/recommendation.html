Workbench is the one to build. Kiwimake is a place where ordinary people get things made for them, and Workbench is the only direction that feels like that place: warm paper, honest materials, a calm room. Almanac feels like reading about apps, Signal feels like a tool for professionals.

Its accent means something. Bottle green is used for exactly two things: the next thing to do (New app, Install, Sign in) and "ready". The moment the product exists for, a new version arriving on the phone, is the one green button on the screen, and the only other colour, brick red for a problem, can never be confused with it. Almanac's blue reads as a link, and Signal's yellow reads as "caution", the opposite of ready.

It reads best where it matters. Bricolage Grotesque has an optical-size axis, so one family gives both the confident display cut and a sturdy text cut that stays clear at 14px in the chat's tool rows on a cheap phone. Almanac's serif agent voice is lovely but heavier on long replies, and its hairline grid fades on low-contrast screens; Signal's uppercase mono labels slow down people who do not read code.

It is the easiest to keep intact. Paper and ink with one green leaves little room for drift: when in doubt, it is ink. Two families (Bricolage Grotesque, IBM Plex Mono), both OFL. Almanac and Signal remain one value swap away in tokens.ts.
